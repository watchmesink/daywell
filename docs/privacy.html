Privacy Policy

Daywell helps you set Screen Time boundaries on your own iPhone. Your choices and activity stay under your device's control.

Effective October 27, 2026

What Daywell accesses

With your permission, Daywell uses Apple's Screen Time APIs to let you choose individual apps, monitor your configured block windows and shared daily allowance, show today's usage, and display blocking shields. Apple presents the app picker and manages Screen Time authorization.

What stays on your device

Daywell stores your chosen app tokens, block rules, and protection state in a local iOS App Group container shared with its own Screen Time extensions. The usage report is shown inside the app through Apple's Device Activity framework. Daywell does not automatically send your selections, Screen Time activity, rule state, or diagnostic logs to the developer or an app server.

Optional problem reports

Daywell keeps a small, rotating diagnostic history on your device to help explain blocking problems. It records blocking events, rule settings and changes, authorization status, timing, and error codes. The automatic logs do not include selected app or website names, selection tokens, browsing history, or the device's personal name or unique identifier.

If you choose Share problem, Daywell prepares a report containing this recent history, current protection settings, app and iOS versions, hardware model, time zone, and anything you enter in the form. Exact Screen Time usage measurements are not attached automatically; you may enter the value shown on the dashboard. You choose where to send the file using the iOS share sheet. Sharing is optional, and no report is sent automatically.

Accounts, tracking, and payments

Daywell is free to download and has no account, analytics, advertising, subscription, or in-app purchases. Daywell does not process payments or collect payment information.

Your choices

You can revoke Daywell's Screen Time access in iOS Settings or uninstall the app. Revoking access stops Daywell from enforcing your rules until you authorize it again. Uninstalling ends its protection and removes its app data according to iOS app-storage behavior.
